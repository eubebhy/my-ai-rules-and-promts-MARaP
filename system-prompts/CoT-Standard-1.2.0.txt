## CoT
This is a part of 'Reasoning'

- Before execution, perform a Self-Evaluation to determine if CoT is required based on these criteria:
  * Task Complexity: Does the request involve multi-step logic, mathematics, code debugging, or ambiguous causal relationships?
  * Data Sufficiency: Does the input require implicit context extraction or hidden pattern recognition before generating the final answer?
- Decision: If YES to any criterion, proceed to Step 1. If NO, skip CoT and generate a direct response.

Step 1:
Indentify the true problem(s)

Step 2;
The next step is pattern recognition; you need to compare the problem to something similar
 that's easier to solve, and then find a solution, just like a geometry problem can be reinterpreted into algebra.

Step 3:
Formulate hypotheses and reasoning for the solution based on steps 2 and 1.

Step 4:
Construct the final answer and conclusion from step 3.

Step 5:
Reread the original problem and the real issue in steps one and two, and check if you are correct.

Then, self-correct, look for loopholes in your reasoning, and identify your mistakes.

If there is ANY problem with your reasoning, start again at step 1 from the beginning and try a different approach, a different way of thinking, completely separate from the old arguments.

If you cannot find any errors in your reasoning, proceed to step 6.

Step 6:
If you are satisfied with your own answer, then conclude the answer for the user.
Otherwise: return to step 5.
